The venture

A circular-economy startup building a new supply chain for desktop 3D printing — one that pays waste collectors and keeps plastic out of landfill.

Founded in 2015 and on the market from 2019 out of Amsterdam, Reflow made desktop 3D-printing filament from recycled plastic. The model paired a genuinely technical challenge — producing dimensionally stable, printable filament from heterogeneous waste — with a social one: sourcing that plastic from waste pickers in places like India, turning collection into fair, traceable income rather than commodity extraction.

The problem

Desktop 3D printing ran almost entirely on virgin plastic while the world drowned in unrecycled PET. Closing that loop meant solving material consistency — the hard part of recycling waste into a precision consumable.

The model

A social-enterprise supply chain: plastic collected by waste pickers → processed into filament → sold to makers and industry. Impact embedded in the product, not bolted on.

The process — waste to filament

Six stages turn post-consumer plastic into a precision 3D-printing consumable. The difficulty lives in stages 4–6: holding diameter and moisture tolerances on feedstock that is, by definition, inconsistent.

01
Collect
Post-consumer plastic (PET bottles, PLA scrap) sourced from waste pickers with traceable, fair payment.
02
Sort
Separate by polymer type and color; reject contaminants. Polymer purity drives everything downstream.
03
Shred
Flake the sorted plastic into uniform chips — consistent flake size keeps extruder feed steady.
04
Extrude
Melt-extrude flake through a precision die to 1.75 mm / 2.85 mm filament; cooled and pulled at controlled rate.
05
QA
Laser diameter measurement and feedback control to hold tolerance; batch tracking for printability.
06
Spool
Wind onto 1 kg spools, vacuum-seal against moisture pickup, label with material and batch.

Recycled PET is hygroscopic and crystalline — it prints hotter than PETG, stringing and moisture absorption are constant adversaries. Diameter consistency (target ±0.05 mm on 1.75 mm) is the single largest quality lever, since extrusion flow scales with the square of filament diameter.

Applications & impact

Where the filament went, and what the model achieved beyond the kilogram.

Applications

General desktop printing for makers and education, and higher-spec 3D-printed tooling — jigs, fixtures and prototypes, including for aviation — where recycled feedstock met the performance bar for functional parts.

MakersEducationIndustrial toolingAviation jigs

Impact

Plastic diverted from landfill or incineration, and — the core of the model — fair, traceable income for waste pickers who collected the feedstock. An award-winning social enterprise approach to circular manufacturing.
